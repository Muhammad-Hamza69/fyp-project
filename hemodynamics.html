Technical Reference

Hemodynamic & Morphology Parameters

CFD and morphological metrics for vascular risk assessment — the quantities the dashboard reports, where each one comes from, and what it means for the vessel wall.

1 Governing physics: blood velocity & pressure

Blood velocity u and pressure p are solved simultaneously from the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. Everything further down this page is derived from that solution — none of it is measured independently.

Continuity — conservation of mass
∇⋅u =0
Momentum — Navier–Stokes
ρ ( ∂u∂t +u⋅∇u ) = −∇p +μ∇2u +f
Symbols, with the value this project actually uses
SymbolQuantityUsed here
u Blood velocity vector field (ux, uy, uz), from the solver solved
p Static pressure distribution, solved simultaneously with u solved
ρ Blood density 1060 kg/m3
μ Dynamic viscosity of blood 0.0035 Pa·s
f External body forces neglected
ρ is not only a physical constant here — it is a unit conversion. OpenFOAM's incompressible solvers work in kinematic units, so wallShearStress comes out in m²/s², not pascals. Every shear value must be multiplied by ρ to become a stress. Miss it and every TAWSS on this site is roughly a thousand times too small, and every low-shear alert fires on every case.

2 The fundamental wall parameter: wall shear stress

Wall shear stress τw is the tangential frictional force flowing blood exerts on the endothelium — the single cell layer lining the vessel. It is the boundary quantity from which every pulsatile index below is derived.

Wall shear stress vector
τw = μ ( ∂u∂n ) wall

n is the wall normal, so ∂u/∂n is how quickly velocity changes as you move away from the wall.

This is why mesh resolution matters most at the wall. WSS is a gradient measured across the very first cell touching the surface. Coarse cells smear that gradient and under-report the stress, which is why the meshes in this project carry prism layers on the wall patches, and why the coarse transient runs used for OSI are explicitly not used for TAWSS.

3 Pulsatile & derived indices

Over a heartbeat of duration T, both the direction and magnitude of the flow change continuously. The indices below integrate that behaviour across the cycle — which is why every one of them requires a transient solve, and why a steady solve produces some of them and not others.

3.1  Time-averaged wall shear stress TAWSS

The mean magnitude of the instantaneous WSS vector over one cycle.

TAWSS= 1T ∫0T |τw(t)| dt

Higher TAWSS

Stronger frictional load on the wall — fast-moving flow.

Lower TAWSS

Stagnation, endothelial dysfunction, wall degeneration and aneurysm growth. Below 0.4 Pa is the threshold this dashboard alerts on.

The magnitude goes inside the integral, not outside. Averaging the WSS vector and then taking its magnitude gives |τw¯|, which is the numerator of OSI — a different quantity that is smaller wherever the flow oscillates. Chain the operations the wrong way round and TAWSS silently reads low precisely in the disturbed regions you care about.

3.2  Oscillatory shear index OSI

How far the WSS vector deviates from, and reverses against, the dominant flow direction.

OSI= 12 ( 1− |∫0Tτw(t)dt| ∫0T|τw(t)|dt )

Bounded:  0 ≤ OSI ≤ 0.5

OSI = 0

Blood flows in strictly one direction for the whole cycle.

OSI = 0.5

The flow reverses so completely that net forward shear is zero. High OSI marks recirculation, vortex formation and rupture-prone regions.

A steady solve cannot produce OSI. It produces exactly zero, by construction. With one flow state, the two integrals are the same vector, the fraction is 1, and OSI is 0 — not because the flow does not oscillate, but because nothing was ever averaged. This is why the dashboard shows n/a rather than 0.00 for a steady case: 0.00 is the most reassuring value on the scale, and it would be a measurement nobody made.

3.3  Relative residence time RRT

How long blood lingers near the wall, coupling low shear magnitude with directional oscillation.

RRT= 1 (1−2⋅OSI)×TAWSS

High RRT

Slow, stagnant flow hugging the wall. This dashboard alerts above 3.0 Pa−1.

Why it matters

Strongly associated with thrombus formation, platelet deposition and aneurysm progression.

RRT must be averaged over the sac, not computed from the sac's averages. It is non-linear in TAWSS and OSI, so by Jensen's inequality the mean of the function is not the function of the means — and because the reciprocal is convex, evaluating it at average shear systematically under-reports residence time. On this cohort the gap was 11.07 against 4.25, 21.35 against 7.10 and 7.17 against 2.94. The last of those sat below the 3.0 alert threshold, so a case that should have been flagged was not.

3.4  Endothelial cell activation potential ECAP

The ratio of flow oscillation to shear magnitude — susceptibility to endothelial damage.

ECAP= OSITAWSS [Pa−1]

Higher ECAP → more endothelial damage → higher rupture risk

ECAP inherits OSI's dependence on a cardiac cycle exactly. On a steady solve it is 0/TAWSS=0 and is just as meaningless, so it is reported as n/a for the same reason.

4 Summary of parameters

NeuroFlow visualises the fundamental flow fields — pressure, velocity, streamlines and WSS — and derives the pulsatile indicators from them.

Parameter Formula / field source Clinical & biomechanical significance
Pressure p Navier–Stokes momentum Hydrostatic pressure distribution; transmural wall forces.
Velocity u Continuity & momentum Streamlines, vortex cores, dynamic flow patterns.
WSS τw μ(∂u/∂n) at the wall Instantaneous endothelial friction vector field.
TAWSS 1T∫|τw|dt Average shear load. Low → stagnation and wall weakening.
OSI 12(1−|∫τw|∫|τw|) Shear reversal, 0–0.5. High → recirculation and vortex.

ECAP OSI/TAWSS Oscillatory-to-shear ratio. High → peak endothelial damage.

The formulas above are standard. This section is not — it records how each one is obtained here, so a reader can tell a solved quantity from an estimated one without reading the source.

Estimated is not solved. The surrogate interpolates between real CFD solutions; it is not a solve of your geometry. A full transient solve of one case takes hours on this hardware, which is the irreducible cost of the physics. Outside the calibrated diameter range the estimate is an extrapolation and is flagged as one on the case.

Solver: OpenFOAM ESI v2412 — simpleFoam (steady), pimpleFoam (transient), snappyHexMesh. Blood modelled as Newtonian, ρ = 1060 kg/m3, μ = 0.0035 Pa·s.
